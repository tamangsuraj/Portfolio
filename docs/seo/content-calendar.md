## Recurring, all year

**Weekly (15 min)** — one Google Business Profile post; check Search Console for
crawl errors.

**Monthly (1 hr)** — review Search Console queries; find pages ranking positions
5–15 and improve them (fastest wins available); update `lastmod` on anything
edited; search your own name.

**Quarterly (half day)** — full `npm run seo:audit` and Lighthouse pass; refresh
the highest-traffic posts; review which keywords moved and why; re-check that
`published: false` routes are either written or removed.

---

## Refreshing beats publishing

By Q4, updating an existing post that ranks position 8 will usually gain you more
traffic than a brand-new post. Google rewards freshness on commercial and
how-to content, and you already have the internal links pointing at it.

Signals a post needs a refresh:
- Ranking 5–15 for its target query (close, needs a push)
- Impressions rising, clicks flat (title and description need rewriting)
- Published over 12 months ago and mentions prices or tools

---

## If you fall behind

Cut in this order, keeping the top of the list:

1. **Service pages** — these convert, nothing else does
2. **Case studies** — proof, and the thing prospects actually read
3. **The original-data study** — one asset, disproportionate link value
4. **Lead magnets**
5. **Blog posts** — genuinely optional in year one
6. **Location pages** — lowest value, highest doorway-page risk

Missing a blog post costs almost nothing. Missing the restaurant service page
costs you the highest-value keyword on the site.
