# 12-Month Backlink & Authority Plan

A new domain with no links ranks for its owner's name and very little else. The
plan below is ordered by *effort-to-value*, not by link volume.

**Two ground rules.** Never buy links or use a "500 backlinks for $20" service —
those are link-spam under Google's policies and the cheapest way to poison a young
domain. And every profile must use the **exact same** name, bio, location and URL,
because entity consolidation is doing most of the work here (see
`personal-brand.md`).

Canonical URL to use everywhere: `https://www.suraj-tamang.com.np`

---

## Phase 1 — Months 1–2: foundation

These are free, permanent, entirely in your control, and should all be done in the
first fortnight.

| Target | Action | Link type |
|---|---|---|
| **GitHub** | Website field on profile; README linking the site; pin the portfolio repo | Nofollow, high entity value |
| **LinkedIn** | Website field, Featured section, About text matching your bio | Nofollow, very high entity value |
| **Google Business Profile** | Create as a service-area business, Kathmandu | Critical for local/Maps |
| **Bing Places** | Mirror the GBP listing | Local |
| **Instagram / Facebook** | Bio link; consistent name and description | Entity |
| **Search Console + Bing Webmaster** | Verify, submit sitemap, request indexing on all 9 live URLs | Indexation |
| **Herald College Kathmandu** | Ask about an alumni profile or student showcase | **.edu.np — highest value link available to you** |
| **Vercel demo sites** | Add a small "Built by Suraj Tamang" footer credit linking back | Direct, relevant |

> The Herald College link is worth more than everything else in this phase
> combined. A `.edu.np` domain carries real trust and almost nobody in your
> position asks for one. Email the department; a project showcase or alumni
> spotlight is a normal request.

## Phase 2 — Months 2–4: developer & professional profiles

Each of these is a real profile you'd maintain anyway. Fill them properly rather
than dumping a link.

- **Dev.to** — republish 3–4 blog posts with `canonical_url` pointing at your site.
  Dev.to supports rel-canonical, so this earns reach without duplicate-content risk.
- **Hashnode** — same, and it lets you map a subdomain.
- **Medium** — same, using their canonical import tool. Medium is the weakest of
  the three for SEO but the strongest for human reach in Nepal.
- **Stack Overflow** — profile with website link; answer Power BI/DAX questions.
- **Behance / Dribbble** — post the five concept builds as design case studies.
  Label them as concept work there too.
- **Product Hunt** — only if you ship an actual tool (a free dashboard template or
  restaurant website checklist would qualify). Don't launch a portfolio.
- **Read.cv / Polywork** — clean professional profiles that rank well for names.

**Republishing rule:** publish on your own site *first*, wait for it to be indexed
(check Search Console), then syndicate with a canonical tag back. Reversing that
order hands the ranking to the platform.

## Phase 3 — Months 3–6: local & directory

Nepali directories vary wildly in quality. Prioritise ones that are actually used
by humans; skip anything that looks like a link farm.

- Google Business Profile posts (weekly, 2 minutes each)
- **Clutch** and **GoodFirms** — freelancer profiles; both rank well for
  "web developer Nepal" style queries
- **Upwork / Fiverr / Freelancer** — profile links, plus genuine lead flow
- Nepali business directories: Nepal Yellow Pages, Biz Serve Nepal, Hamro Bazar
  (business listing, not classifieds)
- **Foodmandu / restaurant community groups** — you worked there; that's a real
  relationship, not cold outreach
- Kathmandu startup and tech Facebook groups — participate, don't spam

**Consistency matters more than volume here.** Name, address (Kathmandu), phone
and website must be byte-identical across every listing. Inconsistent NAP data
actively harms local ranking.

## Phase 4 — Months 4–9: content-earned links

This is where durable authority actually comes from.

**Guest posts.** Target: Nepali business and tech blogs, restaurant industry
sites, BI community blogs. Pitch a specific article, not "can I write for you".
Two or three well-placed guest posts beat twenty directory links.

**Free tools and templates as link magnets.** Genuinely the highest-leverage item
on this page:
- A free **restaurant website checklist** (PDF or interactive page)
- A free **Google Sheets sales dashboard template**
- A **website speed checker for Nepali businesses**

People link to tools. They rarely link to service pages. Host these on
`/resources/`, gate nothing behind email if you want links, and promote them in
the communities above.

**Original data.** If you can publish something like *"I checked the load speed of
50 Kathmandu restaurant websites — here's what I found"*, that is the single most
linkable thing you could produce. It's a weekend of work, it's genuinely useful,
it directly demonstrates your expertise, and local press and business groups pick
up that kind of study.

## Phase 5 — Months 6–12: authority & PR

- Speak at or attend Kathmandu tech meetups; organiser sites usually link speakers
- Podcast or YouTube interviews in the Nepali startup scene
- Open-source a small useful tool (a Power BI theme, a Sheets script) — GitHub
  repos accumulate links naturally
- University guest lecture at Herald College — another `.edu.np` opportunity
- Local news or business publication coverage, using the original-data study as
  the hook

## Targets and honest expectations

| Month | Referring domains | Realistic ranking state |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5–8 | Name queries only |
| 3 | 12–18 | Name queries won; long-tail service terms appearing |
| 6 | 25–35 | Page 1 for several long-tail; page 2–3 for pillar terms |
| 12 | 45–60 | Page 1 for most long-tail; competitive on 2–3 pillar terms |

**Do not expect to rank for "restaurant website Nepal" in month two.** Commercial
terms in an established market take six to twelve months even with everything
right. The long-tail queries — "how much does a restaurant website cost in Nepal"
— are winnable in weeks, convert better, and are how you get the first clients
while the bigger terms mature.

---

## Never do these

- Buy links, or use PBNs, link exchanges or "guest post packages"
- Comment-spam with your URL
- Submit to bulk directories (the "500 directories" services)
- Use exact-match anchor text on every external link — it looks manufactured
- Create fake profiles or reviews

A young domain has no authority to absorb a penalty. One bad link campaign can
cost more than a year of good work.
